Northfield & Co. — E-Commerce & Marketing Performance Analysis
 Project Overview

This project is an end-to-end E-Commerce and Marketing Performance Analysis** for Northfield & Co., an online retail business.

Using 852 days of business data, the analysis explores revenue trends, promotional campaigns, paid media spending, direct-mail performance, affiliate activity, and customer acquisition patterns.

The objective is to identify what drives revenue, how marketing channels perform, and how customer behavior changes across different business periods.


Business Questions

The analysis aims to answer:

* What are the key drivers of revenue?
* How much impact do promotional campaigns have on sales?
* Which paid marketing channels receive the most investment?
* How does direct mail affect other marketing channels?
* Which periods are most effective for acquiring new customers?
* How do promotional and non-promotional periods differ?
* What relationship exists between marketing spend and revenue?

Key Findings
 Revenue Performance

* Total revenue across the 852-day period was approximately $52.90M.
* Black Friday / Cyber Monday (BFCM) campaigns generated a +185.9% lift in daily revenue compared with baseline non-promotional days.
* Promotional periods generated the largest revenue spikes, reaching approximately $300K/day at peak.

 Promotional & Pricing Impact

* Full-price days generated a higher proportion of new-customer revenue (36.2%).
* Promotional days generated significantly higher overall revenue volume.
* This highlights a trade-off between short-term revenue generation and new-customer acquisition.

Marketing Mix

Paid media spending was distributed across 13 channels.

The largest spending allocations were:

| Channel        | Share of Paid Spend |
| -------------- | ------------------: |
| Google PMAX    |               25.4% |
| Google Branded |               24.8% |
| Meta ASC       |               16.9% |

These three channels together accounted for **more than two-thirds of total paid media spending**.

Direct Mail & Affiliate Performance

Direct-mail campaign periods were analyzed to identify potential cross-channel effects.

* Awin affiliate conversions increased by 78.5% during mailing periods.
* Affiliate spend had a strong positive correlation with total revenue (**r = 0.76**).

> Note: Correlation indicates association and does not by itself establish causation.

Customer Acquisition

Customer behavior was analyzed across different day types.

* Mailing periods were more heavily weighted toward returning customers, with only **24.3% new customers**.
* Non-promotional baseline days generated the highest relative share of first-time customers.

This demonstrates that the periods producing the most revenue are not necessarily the same periods producing the highest proportion of new customers.

 Key Business Takeaway

The analysis shows that revenue growth, marketing efficiency, and customer acquisition are not necessarily driven by the same activities.

Promotional campaigns can create substantial short-term revenue increases, while non-promotional periods may generate a greater proportion of new customers. Direct-mail campaigns also coincide with notable changes in affiliate performance, emphasizing the importance of evaluating marketing channels both individually and together.


Project Purpose

This project demonstrates how Python, statistics, visualization, and business reasoning** can be combined to transform raw e-commerce data into insights that support marketing and business decision-making.
